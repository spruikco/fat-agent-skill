Where did the impressions go?

Case 2026-0917

Subject: Australian digital agency · Pages: 1,875 indexable · Symptom: leads stopped

Traffic didn't slide. It fell off a cliff. Weekly Google impressions dropped from about 29,000 to about 1,100 in a single week.

{{CHART}}
  • P1The drop lines up with Google's August 2026 spam update, which began on 18 August. Clicks fell 87% across the update window.
  • OKEvery key page was still "Submitted and indexed". Google had the pages. It had simply stopped showing them.
  • P1674 pages were near-identical templates: suburb and city pages with the place name swapped. Together they earned 57 clicks in 90 days.
  • P2Every suburb page claimed to be run by "local experts" but listed the same interstate phone number.
31Keep
55Improve
462Prune

Verdict: consistent with a doorway and scaled-content demotion. Prune the templates, strengthen the hub pages, and watch for recovery at the next core update.

Triage by page diffing and real Search Console clicks. We also tested a small local AI judge on this site: it made the calls worse, so model judges stay advisory until they prove themselves. Correlation, not a confession: FAT never claims Google penalised you unless Search Console shows a manual action.

Case 2026-1031

Illustrative case · composite figures

Subject: Shopify store, homewares · Pages: 4,812 crawled · Symptom: organic sales down 62%

Sales didn't dip. They vanished. He lined up the usual suspects and questioned every one.

3,400Near-duplicate collection and filter pages, all indexed
1,200Product descriptions copied word for word from the manufacturer
212Products with no price in their structured data
38Internal links to checkout pages that 404

Verdict: the duplicates did it. It's always the duplicates. Consolidate the filter pages, rewrite the copied descriptions, fix the product markup and the dead links. Organic sales were back within two core updates.

Method

Fix. Audit. Test.

Three letters, one routine, and a punch list that remembers what it found.

F

Fix

He doesn't just leave a report on your desk. With your repo open he makes the changes himself: headers, schema, redirects, sitemaps and content briefs.

A

Audit

He crawls the site, runs every relevant module, pulls your Search Console data and ranks what he finds from P0 to P3.

T

Test

After you deploy he re-crawls. Fixed items close themselves. Anything that crept back gets reopened, so nothing slips through twice.

In practice he audits first. But nobody wants to hire an AFT Agent.

Informants

Every good detective has a few.

Some calls need judgement, not pattern matching. Is this suburb page real local content, or the template with the name swapped? Does this page actually answer the question? He asks an informant, and every one answers the same way: a calibrated yes or no.
